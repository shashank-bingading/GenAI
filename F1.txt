FORMULA 1: A QUICK GUIDE
========================

1. WHAT IS FORMULA 1?
---------------------
Formula 1 (F1) is the highest class of international single-seater auto
racing, sanctioned by the FIA (Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile).
The first World Championship season was held in 1950. Today, a season
consists of 20+ races called Grands Prix, held across the world.

2. THE BASICS
-------------
- Teams (Constructors): 10 teams, each running 2 drivers (20 cars total)
- Two championships: the Drivers' Championship and the Constructors' Championship
- Race weekend format: Practice sessions -> Qualifying -> Race
- Sprint weekends add a shorter Saturday race with its own points

3. RACE WEEKEND STRUCTURE
-------------------------
Friday:    Free Practice sessions (teams tune car setups)
Saturday:  Final practice + Qualifying (decides starting grid)
           - Q1: all cars; slowest are eliminated
           - Q2: top 15 continue; slowest eliminated
           - Q3: top 10 fight for pole position
Sunday:    The Grand Prix (roughly 300 km, max about 2 hours)

4. POINTS SYSTEM (STANDARD RACE)
--------------------------------
1st: 25   2nd: 18   3rd: 15   4th: 12   5th: 10
6th: 8    7th: 6    8th: 4    9th: 2    10th: 1
(Sprint races award points to the top 8: 8-7-6-5-4-3-2-1)

5. FLAGS
--------
Green:         Track is clear
Yellow:        Danger ahead, no overtaking
Red:           Session stopped
Blue:          Let a faster car lap you
Checkered:     Session or race finished
Black/Orange:  Driver disqualified / car has mechanical problem

6. THE CAR
----------
- Hybrid power unit: 1.6L V6 turbo + electrical energy recovery systems
- DRS (Drag Reduction System): rear wing flap opens to aid overtaking
- Halo: titanium cockpit protection device
- Tyres by Pirelli: Soft (red), Medium (yellow), Hard (white),
  plus Intermediate (green) and Wet (blue) for rain
- Cars can exceed 350 km/h and corner at over 5 G

7. PIT STOPS
------------
Teams change tyres in about 2-3 seconds. Strategy (when to pit, which
tyres to use) often decides the race result. Drivers must use at least
two different tyre compounds in a dry race.

8. LEGENDARY DRIVERS
--------------------
- Michael Schumacher (7 titles)
- Lewis Hamilton (7 titles)
- Juan Manuel Fangio (5 titles)
- Alain Prost (4 titles)
- Sebastian Vettel (4 titles)
- Max Verstappen (4 titles)
- Ayrton Senna (3 titles, widely regarded as one of the greatest)

9. ICONIC CIRCUITS
------------------
- Monaco (Circuit de Monaco): tight street circuit, the jewel of the calendar
- Silverstone (UK): home of the first-ever F1 race in 1950
- Monza (Italy): "Temple of Speed"
- Spa-Francorchamps (Belgium): famous for Eau Rouge and changing weather
- Suzuka (Japan): figure-eight layout
- Interlagos (Brazil): dramatic championship deciders
- Marina Bay (Singapore): night race

10. FAMOUS TEAMS
----------------
- Ferrari: oldest and most successful team, in F1 since 1950
- McLaren: multiple championships, rich history
- Mercedes: dominant in the hybrid era (2014-2021)
- Red Bull Racing: dominant in the early 2010s and again since 2022
- Williams: legendary British team

11. USEFUL TERMS
----------------
Pole position:  First place on the starting grid
Undercut:       Pitting earlier to gain time on fresh tyres
Overcut:        Staying out longer to gain track position
Box box:        Radio call telling the driver to pit
Parc ferme:     Cars are locked from major changes after qualifying
Fastest lap:    Quickest single lap in the race
Slipstream/Tow: Following closely to reduce drag and gain speed
Lights out:     The race start signal

12. FUN FACTS
-------------
- F1 drivers can lose 2-4 kg in sweat during a hot race.
- A modern F1 car can generate enough downforce to theoretically drive
  upside down at high speed.
- The pit stop world record is under 2 seconds.
- Drivers' heart rates can average 160+ bpm during a race.

Note: Rules, teams, and calendars change season to season, so check
formula1.com for the latest information.
